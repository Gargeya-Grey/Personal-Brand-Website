# UI audit: remote main and personal playground

Reviewed 29 September 2026. These scores are frozen at the start of the audit; the fixes below do not retroactively increase them.

## Comparison and limits

- Production: `origin/main` at `964c524573b8562c8bfecbab0a167e3bc86188c9`, viewed at https://www.sgargeya.com. GitHub's latest Production deployment points to that revision.
- Proposed version: `origin/codex/personal-playground` at `b74b983e2cac291d22e9669799129b4e59e0bc5d`, viewed through its local production build with the same published content. This is a UI comparison, not a deployment-latency comparison.
- Both themes inspected at 1440x1000 and 390x844. Coverage includes home, Journal, a full published article, About, Notes, social destinations, and the branch's Playground. Scrolled article content was inspected; untriggered offscreen entrance animations in full-page captures were not counted as missing content.
- Public experience only. This audit does not rate authenticated workflows, newsletter delivery, article factual accuracy, real-device Safari behavior, or conversion performance.
- The reviewer also authored the branch. A fixed rubric and explicit counterexamples reduce favoritism, but this is an expert self-review, not an independent blind study. Treat differences smaller than one point cautiously.

## Rubric

Each dimension is a holistic 0-10 judgment, rounded to half points. No composite score: the three dimensions answer different questions.

- **AI-slop feel (lower is better):** interchangeable copy; template repetition; decoration without information; imagery disconnected from actual work. This describes how generic the interface feels, not whether AI made it. An individual gradient, serif, or AI-made image is not inherently a flaw.
- **Premium UI (higher is better):** typography and hierarchy; restrained composition; coherent surfaces and contrast; responsive finishing and interaction details. 5 = usable with conspicuous rough edges; 7 = coherent with visible compromises; 9 = highly resolved throughout.
- **Ease of understanding (higher is better):** identifying the person and purpose; predicting link destinations; reaching content; readable controls and restrained calls to action. These are observed interface characteristics, not measured user comprehension.

### Why main scores this way

The repeated rounded gradient hero, nested mini-browser illustrations, status pills, section kickers, and shadowed cards make unrelated destinations feel like copies of one SaaS landing page. Phrases such as “Architecting Intelligence. Curating ART.” and “high-signal work” consume attention without immediately explaining the person or the destination. The homepage repeats Edudojo in the hero, working index, featured work, and closing CTA. The Journal adds a mini archive and statistics before the full article cards. Its three hero article links are functional, and are a useful shortcut for returning readers.

The light mode's translucent mint surfaces and broad shadows flatten hierarchy. Dark mode separates the surfaces more convincingly, though the repeated glow/card pattern remains. Changing theme does not solve the information architecture, so clarity receives the same score.

Main's strengths are real: sturdy sans-serif article headlines, readable long-form body typography, explicit Edudojo links, above-the-fold Journal article links, and an established article table of contents. Those should survive a redesign.

### Why the branch is better, but not finished

A prominent real portrait, flatter surfaces, a coherent artwork-derived palette, a functional experiment, and simpler social links make the branch feel more personal. It brings full article summaries and artwork higher on the Journal page, although main already offers clickable article titles in its hero. Dark mode's navy surfaces and mint details fit the journal artwork especially well.

However, “A curious mind. A work in progress.” and the repeated rhetorical section titles remain interchangeable portfolio copy. Most index pages repeat the same giant serif introduction with one italic accent. The thin display serif also leaked into long article titles, where main's type was easier to scan. The collage and oversized footer wordmark are familiar design conventions, not evidence of originality on their own. The light palette is clean but can feel uniformly pale across large stretches.

Both versions repeat subscription requests. Before this audit, Notes has three forms and Journal has two. The article body itself is substantially shared between versions, so the branch does not get credit for reinventing that reading experience.

## Still preventing a 9/10

1. **Evidence of actual work.** The project collection still has atmospheric artwork and broad descriptions. Product screenshots, a short explanation of a design decision, and one concrete outcome per project would contribute more credibility. Do not invent results or substitute decorative mockups for evidence.
2. **Depth of the playground.** One small idea mixer demonstrates the mechanism, but a richer collection of useful or surprising experiments is needed to reward repeat visits. That requires distinct projects, not more decorative cards.
3. **Personal specificity.** The site needs more first-person particulars only Gargeya can supply: what changed his mind, a tradeoff he made, an unfinished question, or the story behind a film. The audit removes generic filler without fabricating those details.
4. **Independent comprehension testing.** Ask unfamiliar visitors what Gargeya does after ten seconds, then ask them to find an app, an essay, and a social profile. Record hesitation and wrong turns. The current ratings cannot substitute for those observations.
